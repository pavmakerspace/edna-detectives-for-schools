Explore: How eDNA works

Reading the clues life leaves behind

Living things leave tiny pieces of DNA in their surroundings through skin, mucus, waste and other material. This is environmental DNA (eDNA). Scientists can study a sample and compare its DNA with a library of DNA from known species (types of living things). A match can be a clue to life they cannot see.

01

Shed

An animal leaves traces of its DNA in the water and on the bed below.

02

Collect

Scientists collect a sample of water or mud, noting its depth and place.

03

Read

They separate the DNA from the sample and compare it with DNA in a library of known species.

Task 3: The evidence challenge

How sure can you be?

Watch What is eDNA. How does finding a DNA trace differ from seeing an animal? Discuss your answer before considering the case below.

Scientists collect a water sample downstream (in the direction the water flows) from a protected habitat. Its DNA matches a rare fish in their DNA library. A facility upstream also uses that fish as food. For this case, assume the DNA match is correct.

Label each statement S (the evidence supports it), P (it could explain the result), or N (the evidence does not tell us).

___ DNA matching the fish was detected.   ___ A live fish is in the protected habitat now.
___ The DNA could have come from food.   ___ Exactly three fish live in the habitat.

Choose an explanation that could fit the evidence. How sure are you: not very sure, somewhat sure, or very sure? Why?

What other sample or check could help you work out which explanation is right?
